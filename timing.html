When to give the second dose to adult shelter animals

Every adult is assumed to get one dose of modified live vaccine on the day of intake. This tool asks what happens if the second dose moves earlier or later than day 14. A later dose is given to fewer animals, because many have left, and prevents fewer cases, because it acts later. Pick a day and see both sides of that trade for your own stays and window cases. Immunology · from the literature

Susceptible at intake is fixed at the paper's value for the disease chosen, 21%. It cancels out of the doses per case, because a shelter with fewer susceptible adults records the same window cases at a higher daily risk.

Of the animals whose first dose failed, the share a second dose converts. It has never been measured in cats. The default, 25%, is the paper's judgment between a canine cohort, in which a second dose converted 2 of 11 first-dose failures of all ages and none of 8 dogs at least 16 weeks old, and human measles revaccination data, 46% to 77%. Doses per case scale as 1 divided by this share. The model applies the same share whichever day the second dose is given, so the share does not change which day looks better. Whether it changes with the interval between doses has not been measured.

Incubation period, 5 days for panleukopenia and parvovirus and 10 for distemper by default. It sets how much of a count taken on days 8 to 21 comes from animals infected before the intake dose protected them.

Applies to both doses. The paper uses 7 and tests 3 to 14.

Timing in practice. Current protocols give the first dose on intake and hold the second to a minimum interval of about two weeks, because of concerns about immune tolerance. Keeping adults on the same two-week interval as the kitten series also avoids running two schedules. Read days before 14 here as a check on the model rather than a recommendation.

A few concepts

Doses per case averted. How many second doses a shelter gives to prevent one case in an adult from infection acquired in the shelter, including cases diagnosed after the animal leaves. It equals the second doses given divided by the cases they avert, and cost per case averted is that number times the cost of a dose. Only protection of the vaccinated animal is counted.

When a later dose is cheaper per case. It depends on how much time at risk the animals present on each day have left. In the paper's stays, animals still present on a later day are, on average, the ones staying longest, and those are the animals with the most time left at risk. A later dose skips the many short stays, so each dose is better targeted. It also leaves the remaining animals unprotected for longer and reaches fewer of them, so it prevents fewer cases in total. In the paper, cases averted fell from day 7 to day 42 on every stay tested. Doses per case fell on most of them and stayed flat on the rest. With a single group of adults leaving at a constant daily rate, every adult present has the same expected time left whatever the day, so the day makes no difference to doses per case. It can also go the other way. If every adult stayed exactly 30 days, a day-14 dose protecting on day 21 would cover 9 days and a day-21 dose only 2, for the same number of doses, so the later day would need 4.5 times as many doses per case. Uploaded records can do either, which is why the charts are drawn from your stays. A lower number on the third chart is therefore not a reason on its own to move the dose later.

Window case rate. Adults diagnosed 8 to 21 days after intake, per 1,000 days of adult care on days 8 to 21. Animals ill in the first week were mostly infected before arrival, and at the default 7 days to protection a second dose on day 14 cannot act before day 22, so this window counts the same whether or not a shelter already gives one. Count over several years, five if records and the intake protocol allow. The tool turns it into a daily risk for a susceptible adult and holds that risk fixed as the second-dose day moves. Cases never tested are missed, so treat it as approximate. Above a daily risk of about 0.5% the model, a linear approximation, understates the doses per case.

Stays. Upload records reads intake and outcome dates in your browser, and nothing is sent anywhere. Adults are 1 year or older, stays are whole days with intake as day 0, and stays over a year are capped at 365 days. Illustrative stays are built from groups of adults that each leave at a constant daily rate. The stay pattern decides who is still present on each day. A second dose on day T reaches the adults who stay longer than T and protects them for their days of care after day T plus the onset.
